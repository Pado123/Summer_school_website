Local Organizers

  • Massimiliano de Leoni

    Associate Professor of Computer Science, Department of Mathematics, University of Padova, Italy

    Dr. Massimiliano de Leoni is an Associate Professor of Computer Science at the University of Padua (UniPd), Italy, where he leads a research group on Process Science. He obtained a Ph.D. in Computer Engineering in 2009 at SAPIENZA - University of Rome on a thesis on Business Process Flexibility and Adaptation. From 2014 to 2018, he was an Assistant Professor at Eindhoven University of Technology (TU/e), the Netherlands. He is currently the UniPd’s coordinator of the EU-funded Erasmus Mundus BDMA – Big Data Management and Analytics. He is a member of the IEEE Task Force on Process Mining. The research areas of Prof. de Leoni include Business Process Management and Modeling, Process Mining, Business Process Improvement, Process Simulation, Predictive Analytics, and Process-Aware Recommender Systems.

    Email: deleoni@math.unipd.it
    Web: https://www.math.unipd.it/~deleoni/index.html

  • Francesco Vinci

    Department of Mathematics, University of Padova, Italy

    Francesco Vinci is a Postdoctoral Researcher in the Department of Mathematics at the University of Padova, where he did his PhD in the Brain, Mind & Computer Science (BMCS) program. His research focuses on Business Process Simulation: discovering accurate and interpretable simulation models from event data, keeping them aligned with processes that evolve over time, and using them for “what-if” analyses that assess the impact of changes without disrupting real-world operations. His work also covers process-model repair driven by simulation, and applications to public administration and healthcare.

    Email: francesco.vinci@unipd.it

  • Alessandro Padella

    Department of Mathematics, University of Padova, Italy

    Alessandro is a Postdoctoral Researcher at the University of Padova, where he did his PhD in the Brain, Mind & Computer Science (BMCS) program. His research focuses on Responsible Predictive and Prescriptive Process Analytics, emphasis on Fairness and Explanation: predicting how running process instances will unfold, recommending the actions that improve their outcome, and ensuring that these predictions and recommendations are fair towards the people involved and understandable to process stakeholders.

    Email: alessandro.padella@unipd.it
    Web: https://pado123.github.io/

  • Alessandro Galeazzi

    Department of Mathematics, University of Padova, Italy

    Alessandro Galeazzi is a Tenure Track Assistant Professor at the Department of Mathematics, University of Padova. His research interests are in the area of computational social science and network science, with a focus on echo chambers, political polarization, misinformation, algorithmic auditing, and the evolution of cultural artifacts and the online information ecosystems.

    Email: alessandro.galeazzi@math.unipd.it
    Web: https://research.unipd.it/cris/rp/rp97583
